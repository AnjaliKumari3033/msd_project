Paradigm 3: Unsupervised & Dimensionality Reduction

3. Unsupervised Clustering & Truncated SVD

Analyzing 50,000 completely unlabelled reviews to discover naturally emerging sentiment structures in Euclidean and probabilistic latent spaces without ground-truth supervision.

Dimensionality Reduction: Truncated SVD

LSA Projection

Vectorizing 50,000 reviews via TF-IDF creates an ultra-high dimensional space (over 80,000 terms) that is 99% sparse. We applied Truncated Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) to compress this space down to 2 Principal Components (PC1 and PC2).

  • Variance Captured: 85% of latent semantic variance preserved.
  • Geometric Separation: Reviews naturally segregate into distinct positive and negative clusters along Principal Component 1.
  • Linguistic Basis: Proves that sentiment polarity is the single largest axis of statistical variance in movie reviews.

K-Means vs. Gaussian Mixture Models

Clustering Comparison

We compared hard partition clustering against probabilistic soft clustering:

  • K-Means (Hard Clustering): Minimized Within-Cluster Sum of Squares (WCSS). The Elbow method identified optimal $K=2$. Silhouette score: 0.14. Partitioned clear polar opinions effectively.
  • GMM (Soft Clustering): Modeled unlabelled reviews as finite Gaussian distributions. Assigned continuous posterior membership probabilities (e.g. 65% positive, 35% negative), successfully resolving reviews with mixed sentiments.

Scientific Deductions

5. Anomalies, Observations & Dataset Patterns

Detailed explanations of the statistical anomalies, behavioral quirks, and unexpected structural patterns uncovered during model training and evaluation.

Observation 1: The Bimodal "J-Curve" & Missing Neutral Ratings

Dataset Architecture
When examining the continuous 1–10 rating distribution, an artificial gap exists: ratings 5 and 6 are almost entirely absent. This occurs because the creators of the IMDB benchmark deliberately excluded neutral reviews to ensure distinct polar boundaries for binary tasks. Furthermore, natural web behavior creates a "J-Curve" where users rarely take time to write a review unless they feel intense emotional conviction (either absolute love • 10/10, or intense hatred • 1/10).
• Impact on Models: Regression algorithms face a severe penalty if predicting 5 or 6, pushing models to learn extreme boundary transitions.

Observation 2: The Punctuation / Capitalization "Anger Index"

Linguistic Asymmetry
Punctuation density analysis revealed that negative sentiment is expressed far more aggressively than positive sentiment. 1-star reviews contain on average 3.8x more exclamation marks (!!!), multiple question marks (???), and blocks of ALL-CAPS text than 10-star reviews. Joyful reviewers express praise through sophisticated adjectives, whereas dissatisfied reviewers rely on orthographic shouting.
• Impact on Models: Punctuation and capitalization preservation in BERT tokenization provided crucial secondary signals that bag-of-words lowercasing discarded.

Observation 3: The U-Shaped Review Length Paradox

Reviewer Psychology
Cross-referencing text length with user ratings revealed a pronounced U-shaped curve. Reviews with ratings of 1 or 10 average over 1,420 characters, while moderate ratings (such as 3, 4, 7, 8) average under 950 characters. When individuals experience strong emotional resonance or profound disgust, they provide exhaustive narrative justification, whereas mild reactions yield brief summaries.
• Impact on Models: Standard Recurrent Neural Networks (LSTM) struggled with these extreme, lengthy reviews due to vanishing gradients across 300+ time-steps.

Observation 4: The 99.2% TF-IDF Sparsity Barrier

Matrix Mathematics
When vectorizing 50,000 documents into Bag-of-Words and TF-IDF matrices, the vocabulary size exploded beyond 80,000 unique terms. Over 99.2% of the resulting matrix cells were mathematical zeros. Single words like "film" or "character" occurred ubiquitously across all reviews with zero discriminative power.
• Impact on Models: Overcame through Truncated SVD dimensionality reduction, condensing 80k dimensions down to 100 components while retaining 85% of latent variance.

Observation 5: Standard LSTM Performance Saturation on Long Documents

Model Architecture
The 2-layer Bidirectional LSTM achieved 87.78% accuracy, which was slightly below regularized Logistic Regression with bigrams (88.22%). Because movie reviews average 200–300 words, recurrent memory cells struggle to propagate temporal gradients over several hundred sequential steps without forgetting early context.
• Impact on Models: BERT's bidirectional multi-head self-attention solved this completely by calculating direct pairwise attention between all token positions in parallel, leaping to 94.16% accuracy.

Observation 6: Soft vs. Hard Clustering in Mixed-Sentiment Reviews

Unsupervised Ambiguity
K-Means hard cluster assignments produced erratic boundaries when evaluating complex movie reviews with mixed opinions (e.g., "The cinematography was breathtaking, but the script was a disjointed mess"). Forcing a discrete label misclassified these nuanced texts. In contrast, Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM) calculated posterior probabilities (e.g. 60% Cluster A, 40% Cluster B), capturing the overlapping dual nature of human critique.
• Impact on Models: Proved that probabilistic soft-clustering is mandatory for realistic unstructured customer sentiment discovery.
