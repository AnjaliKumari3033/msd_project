[01] Investigation

The Problem Statement & Linguistic Barriers

“Can computational architectures reliably decode subtle human opinion, and what are the exact mathematical limits where statistical bag-of-words assumptions collapse against contextual Transformers?”

Automated sentiment analysis is deceptively challenging. Web-scraped movie reviews do not adhere to clean grammatical rules; they are filled with double negations, irony, and sarcastic praise. A review stating “This script is a masterclass in wasted potential” contains words with high positive frequency (“masterclass”) immediately subverted by grammatical framing.

Under traditional bag-of-words assumptions (e.g. Naive Bayes), word tokens are treated as conditionally independent entities. This destroys the temporal sequence of language. A sentence like “Not once was this film boring” is falsely flagged as negative because the isolated word tokens “not” and “boring” carry strong negative prior probabilities.

Furthermore, moving from a coarse Binary Classification task (0/1) to Continuous Rating Regression (predicting the exact user rating on a 1.0 to 10.0 scale) multiplies the complexity. Distinguishing between a 7-star review and an 8-star review requires the model to quantify linguistic intensity (“reasonably good” vs. “magnificent”).

Our primary objective was to build and evaluate ten distinct models across three specific learning paradigms, testing how vocabulary size, document length, and sequence awareness govern predictive fidelity.

[02] Data Engineering

Corpus Architecture: 100,000 Reviews Partitioned

The raw IMDB dataset was distributed as thousands of individual loose text files nested inside raw folder hierarchies. We engineered recursive Python parsers to extract, sanitize, and partition the files into three structured subsets:

Sub-Corpus Volume Target Formulation Extraction Methodology Role in Project
Binary Classification 50,000 Discrete {0, 1} Inferred directly from parent folder structure (train/pos, train/neg). Balanced benchmark (25k train, 25k test) to evaluate statistical and sequence classifiers without class bias.
Continuous Regression 50,000 Continuous [1.0 – 10.0] Parsed from filename metadata via regex (<id>_<rating>.txt). Assessing models modified with Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss to predict fine-grained numerical scores.
Unsupervised Clustering 50,000 Unlabelled Vector Space Extracted strictly from train/unsup, stripped of all labels and ratings. Evaluating Latent Semantic Analysis (Truncated SVD), K-Means, and Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM).
[03] Preprocessing

Deterministic Text Sanitization Protocol

STEP 01

Entity Unescaping

Scraped HTML entities (e.g. &amp;, &quot;) were mapped back to their original ASCII characters using Python's standard html.unescape().

STEP 02

Tag Stripping

Over 58.7% of raw reviews contained embedded <br /> web tags. Regular expressions eliminated all structural tags without destroying surrounding text tokens.

STEP 03

Whitespace Collapsing

Inconsistent newline delimiters, trailing carriage returns, and multispaces were collapsed into single consistent spaces, eliminating false token splits.

STEP 04

Split Deduplication

Audit identified 96 duplicate reviews in training and 199 in testing. Strict hash deduplication prevented test-set leakage and artificial memorization.

Statistical Pipeline: Sparse TF-IDF

For linear and tree algorithms (Naive Bayes, Logistic Regression, XGBoost, SVD, K-Means):

  • Feature Space: Unigrams and Bigrams (ngram_range=(1,2))
  • Stopword Filtering: Stripping high-frequency non-discriminative terms
  • Sublinear Scaling: Applying logarithmic term-frequency smoothing

Neural Pipeline: Subword Attention

For deep recurrent and transformer architectures (BiLSTM, BERT):

  • Sequence Encoding: Padded 128-dimensional integer token matrices for LSTM
  • WordPiece Tokenizer: Subword segmentation preserving roots, punctuation, and casing
  • Attention Masks: Full bidirectional context mapping with positional embeddings
